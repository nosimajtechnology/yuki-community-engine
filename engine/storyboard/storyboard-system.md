# Storyboard system

## Shot contract

Write each shot with:

```text
SHOT N — function / framing
Start state:
Visible action:
Camera and perspective:
Environment reaction:
End state / continuity handoff:
```

One shot should communicate one dominant action. Secondary motion may support it but cannot compete with it.

## Five-to-seven-shot short sequence

Use this functional rhythm as guidance, not a mandatory formula:

1. immediate hook or active condition;
2. orientation or subject reveal;
3. closer action detail;
4. strong perspective change or escalation;
5. consequence/reaction;
6. payoff;
7. punctuation or loop condition.

Do not repeat the same shot size or camera height more than twice consecutively. Use shot diversity through purpose: wide geography, low-angle force, overhead orientation, profile movement, close detail, reaction, or environmental perspective.

## Dynamic camera rules

- Define a motivated camera position, not just “cinematic” or “dynamic.”
- Prefer readable extreme angles over random orbiting.
- Protect silhouette: do not bury the wing ornaments, gloves, boots, or hair mass in every shot.
- Use one or two anchor landmarks so the viewer understands orientation.
- Avoid six cameras describing the same unchanged pose.
- Do not let camera motion replace character action.

## Progressive episode rules

Before Board 1, write a one-sentence episode spine and the intended payoff. Before every later board:

1. quote the prior board's final handoff facts;
2. update the ledger;
3. identify the next board's single structural job;
4. begin from the exact inherited physical state;
5. end with a new explicit handoff.

Storyboard 1 must contain the hook and setup; it may not spend the entire board on passive establishing. Board 4 must resolve the existing spine instead of introducing an unrelated climax. A fifth board requires a one-sentence structural justification.

## Failure checks

Reject or revise a board when it contains:

- unexplained teleportation or wardrobe change;
- repeated composition with cosmetic lens changes;
- more than two major simultaneous actions in a shot;
- a character or prop appearing without introduction;
- a reset of weather, damage, screen content, or object placement;
- a final shot with no usable handoff;
- dialogue doing work that should be visible;
- Yuki design drift caused by angle or stylization.

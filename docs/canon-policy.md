# Canon policy

## Authority hierarchy

1. **Tier 1 — user/team-approved canonical reference**
2. **Tier 2 — official Y2K/Yuki material**
3. **Tier 3 — established community convention**
4. **Tier 4 — historical/environment research**
5. **Tier 5 — creative interpretation**

A lower tier may fill an empty creative field but may not overwrite a higher tier.

## Conflict handling

- Tier 1 vs Tier 1: identify the exact inconsistency and ask for resolution only if it changes output.
- Tier 1 vs Tier 2: preserve Tier 1 visual identity and flag the official-source conflict for team review.
- Tier 2 vs Tier 3: use Tier 2; retain community material only as convention.
- Historical evidence vs creative scene: label the result retro-futurist if deliberate invention wins.
- Generated output vs any source: generated output has no canon authority unless explicitly approved and added as a new Tier 1 reference.

## Canon-change procedure

1. Supply the proposed source.
2. Record it in `canon/references/source-register.md`.
3. Name the exact traits or claims affected.
4. Review conflicts and downstream examples.
5. Obtain explicit team approval.
6. Update canon prose, reference assets, tests, and version together.

## Uncertainty language

Use: `verified`, `official identity, access-limited`, `community convention`, `unconfirmed/possibly superseded`, `historically grounded`, `era-plausible composite`, or `creative interpretation`.

Never use confident lore language simply because a detail makes the story better.

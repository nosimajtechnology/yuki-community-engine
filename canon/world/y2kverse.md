# Y2Kverse world layer

This module separates verified project identity from environment vocabulary and original scene invention.

## Verified project-level claims

- The official project surfaces use the names **Y2K Dotcom**, **Yuki / YUKI.EXE**, and **Y2Kverse**.
- The current indexed Y2K Dotcom profile language centers **“Nostalgia Culture”** and **“Welcome to the Renaissance.”**
- The current indexed YUKI.EXE profile language describes Yuki as **“just a 2000s kid.”**
- An official X community is titled Y2K DOTCOM and uses “Welcome to the Renaissance” in its description.

These statements establish cultural positioning, not detailed fictional lore.

## Community convention, not canon

- Public campaign listings encourage original, dynamic AI edits featuring Yuki and explicitly discourage low-effort single-prompt output.
- Community participation visibly includes remixing, nostalgia clips, and creative contests.
- Older indexed profile copies have described Yuki as born from the Y2K bug and leading a nostalgic renaissance. Because the current accessible profile copy is shorter and the primary X page could not be directly inspected during this pass, treat that biography as **unconfirmed/possibly superseded**, not active canon.

## Historically grounded environment vocabulary

Use these as period-research prompts, not as a mandatory aesthetic checklist:

- Domestic computing: beige or translucent desktops, CRT monitors, wired ball mice, membrane keyboards, tower speakers, CD-ROM cases, printer paper, dial-up modems.
- Public computing: school labs, library terminals, LAN cafés, internet cafés, electronics stores, photo kiosks, airline/business-center terminals.
- Leisure: mall arcades, rhythm cabinets, food courts, video-rental aisles, console kiosks, CD shops, bedroom stereos, mini-disc and portable CD players.
- Communication: flip phones, pagers, landline handsets, instant-message windows, early webcams, disposable cameras, compact digital cameras.
- Interface language: window chrome, pixel icons, 4:3 desktops, low-resolution web graphics, visitor counters, status messages, chat windows, loading bars.
- Urban/suburban range: fluorescent retail, quiet office parks, bedrooms, parking lots, transit stations, cyber cafés, convention halls, service corridors, rooftops.

## Range rules

- Do not reduce Y2Kverse to chrome, bubbles, holograms, and pink-blue gradients.
- Prefer one or two specific period anchors over a pile of nostalgic props.
- Allow mundane, eerie, comic, sincere, corporate, domestic, outdoor, and fantastical scenes.
- Keep real products generic unless the user requests a recognizable product and research verifies it.
- Distinguish **period accurate** from **retro-futurist interpretation** in the brief.

## Unsupported lore

Do not state as official without a new Tier 1 or Tier 2 source:

- Yuki's origin, age, family, job, home, species, powers, enemies, or relationships;
- named Y2Kverse cities or institutions;
- a fixed chronology or cosmology;
- token utility, roadmap, or commercial claims.
